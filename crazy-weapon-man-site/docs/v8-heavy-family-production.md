# Crazy Weapon Man v8 — Heavy Family Production Checklist

Target branch: `crazy-weapon-man-web`
Visual target: illustrated neon-sewer action game; heavy weapons must feel massive, readable, and physically integrated with Crazy Weapon Man.

## Heavy family scope

Primary family: `heavy`
Current covered gameplay weapon types:
- hammer
- maul-like heavy melee variants
- oversized industrial weapons generated by naming/modifier logic

The heavy family should also visually support artillery-style names and heavy attachments such as cannons, mortars, pile drivers, launchers, welded cleavers, pressure tools, trash compactors, and municipal junk hardware.

## Character animation contract

Required clips for the heavy-family hero set:

- `idle_heavy` — 6 frames
- `run_heavy` — 8 frames
- `jump_heavy` — 3 frames
- `fall_heavy` — 2 frames
- `land_heavy` — 3 frames
- `attack_heavy_a` — 7 frames; horizontal/diagonal committed swing
- `attack_heavy_b` — 7 frames; overhead or reverse swing
- `attack_heavy_air` — 6 frames; downward drop/crush
- `skill_heavy` — 8 frames; ground-zero / artillery discharge
- `hit_heavy` — 3 frames
- `death_heavy` — 7 frames
- `victory_heavy` — 6 frames

Recommended source frame box: 320×320 px.
Runtime visual target: 84–102 px hero height before frenzy scaling.
Weapon may extend well outside the body frame; do not crop weapon tips, barrels, impact debris, or recoil arcs.

## Pose language

Heavy locomotion must read differently from blade/caster/ranged families:

- lower center of gravity
- wider stance
- two-handed bracing on oversized weapons
- visible anticipation before swings
- body recoil after impact/fire
- shorter, heavier run stride
- jump poses pull weapon mass inward
- airborne attack clearly telegraphs downward momentum

The hero body and weapon must be authored as one coordinated action. Socketed fallback rendering is allowed only when a named weapon has no bespoke heavy component available.

## Required anchors per frame

Every frame metadata entry must include normalized anchors:

- `feet`
- `center`
- `head`
- `hand_primary`
- `hand_support`
- `weapon_socket`
- `fx_origin`
- `muzzle` for artillery/firearm-style heavy variants
- `impact_origin` for hammer/maul/pile-driver variants

## Heavy base silhouettes

Reusable illustrated base components should cover at least these silhouettes:

1. industrial maul / trash hammer
2. pile driver
3. junk cannon
4. scrap mortar
5. rotary heavy gun
6. welded cleaver / crusher
7. landfill lobber / launcher
8. pressure propeller / industrial blower
9. siege toaster / appliance weapon
10. trash-compactor block weapon

These are reusable art families, not hard-coded weapon names. Naming/modifier logic should select and decorate them deterministically.

## Naming-system visual modifiers

The heavy art must visibly respond to the existing v8 naming system:

- `Budget` → duct tape, mismatched fasteners, salvaged handle
- `Microwave-Adjacent` / `Microwaved` → exposed coils, vents, warped metal, green/magenta heat glow
- `Tax-Deductible` / accounting names → receipt tags, stamps, paper strips, municipal labels
- `Warranty-Void` / OSHA names → warning tape, exposed hazard components, red warning marks
- `After-Hours` → cool neon accents and night-shift practical lights
- `Violent Payroll` / payroll names → red HR/payroll tags and office-hardware details
- `Questionably Sentient` / sentient traits → eye, teeth, fleshy cable/organic details
- `Relentless` → extra barrels, reinforcement, larger silhouette
- `Living` → organic mouth/eye growth layered over industrial chassis
- `Haunted` → cyan ghost flame, floating bolts/parts
- `Gravitationally Incorrect` / non-Euclidean → disconnected floating weapon segments with orbital FX
- `Fat` → wider chassis and heavier impact shape
- `Tiny` → compressed body, absurdly dense hardware
- `Long` → extended barrel/shaft with adjusted sockets

## Element overlays

Each heavy silhouette must accept a non-destructive element layer:

Positive:
- Fire — heat bloom, orange edge, embers
- Poison — corrosive slime, bubbling green residue
- Lightning — cyan/white arcs and charged hardware
- Water — pressure spray, blue wet highlights
- Earth — rock/rubble plates and impact dust

Negative:
- Backfire — unstable red-orange combustion leaking toward the hero
- Toxic Leak — self-dripping corrosive residue
- Backfeed — electrical feedback cable to grip/hero side
- Undertow — reverse pressure flow / water pull
- Faultline — cracked casing, seismic vibration and loose debris

Element overlays must preserve silhouette readability and never replace the core weapon material treatment.

## Rarity language

Rarity should be visible without requiring UI text:

- Training/Common — plain industrial construction
- Uncommon/Magic — modest trim/accent
- Rare/Super Rare — cleaner edge light and one ornamental feature
- Epic/Heroic — stronger emissive accents and layered hardware
- Legendary/Mythic — dramatic silhouette additions and animated glow
- Exotic/Relic/Ancient — unique material language / floating components
- Transcendent and above — deliberate absurdity, but still readable at gameplay scale

Rarity must not simply mean “more glow.” It should affect shape complexity, ornamentation, and secondary components.

## Runtime integration rules

1. Determine family from current weapon type.
2. Heavy family selects the heavy hero animation set.
3. Name/material/rarity/element generate a deterministic weapon visual descriptor.
4. Descriptor selects a heavy base silhouette plus modifier overlays.
5. Frame metadata places weapon using `weapon_socket`, `hand_primary`, and `hand_support`.
6. Impact/muzzle FX use frame-specific origins.
7. Contact shadow remains a separate runtime layer.
8. Sludge bounce light and local attack light are applied after body/weapon draw.
9. Do not use generic oval clipping masks for completed heavy frames.
10. Gameplay hitboxes remain authored separately from visual bounds.

## Shadow / grounding contract

Heavy family needs stronger grounding than other families:

- idle/run contact shadow: 70–85% of foot-span width
- windup/attack shadow widens slightly during plant frames
- jump/fall shadow shrinks and softens with height
- ground-smash frames add a brief darker impact contact shadow
- toxic sewer bounce light should illuminate lower boots/weapon underside subtly
- artillery muzzle flashes may cast a one-frame warm local light onto hero and nearby geometry

## Attack presentation

Basic heavy melee:
- no default green strike
- use weapon-authored motion + small white/rarity-tinted edge trail
- impact FX only at strike frame

Artillery/heavy firearm:
- visible recoil on hero frame
- muzzle flash at `muzzle`
- projectile starts at the same anchor
- shell casing/debris optional

Ground smash:
- impact at `impact_origin`
- localized dust/debris/shock ring
- no full-screen bloom

## Production acceptance checklist

Heavy pass is complete when:

- [ ] all 12 required clips exist
- [ ] no weapon is clipped at any attack extreme
- [ ] hero hands align with the weapon in idle/run/jump/attack
- [ ] two-handed poses visibly brace heavy weapons
- [ ] hammer/maul and artillery silhouettes feel different
- [ ] name modifiers visibly change the weapon
- [ ] all 10 elements/corruptions layer cleanly
- [ ] rarity treatments are readable without relying only on glow
- [ ] no rectangular crop/background is visible
- [ ] contact shadows and toxic bounce light integrate hero with sewer floor/platforms
- [ ] muzzle and impact anchors line up with FX
- [ ] gameplay hitboxes are unchanged
- [ ] mobile landscape remains readable
